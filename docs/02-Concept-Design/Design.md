The goal of our project is to create an autonomous robot that can inspect 4inch diameter pipes and report any faults it finds. These faults include debris in the pipe, water leakage, or collapsed sections. The robot needs to be able to survey the straight line conduit reliably and completely autonomously to free up workers to finish other tasks.
Our audience is Sundt construction who has shown interest in this specific design and has highlighted its ideal use cases for us. They have specific parameters they want met such as the pipe diameter, pipe length and expected obstacles. We are also building with the individual workers in mind as we want to make operating the robot as easy as possible and not disrupt their workflow on the jobsite. While Sundt has shown interest in the project its uses are for any other construction or inspection company as well. We are also building the project with the EGR 314 teaching staff in mind as this will also serve as a demonstration of our engineering skills.

## Design Concept

![Hello](/assets/DreamMachine.png)
Our concept sketch shows an ideal design for the task Sundt has asked of us. We decided that using treads would be the optimal system for moving through the pipes as they would be able to easily traverse over any small pieces of debris that might be in the pipe without getting the robot stuck. It also allows for the robot to turn in place should it be in a large enough pipe or possibly turn around corners. The large high resolution camera in the sketch would be the “eyes” of our robot as it would be the primary method of fault detection in the pipe using onboard machine learning to recognize cracks or blockages in the pipe. This would be supported by an array of LEDs which will light up the pipe in order for the robot to have the best possible view of the pipe interior. To keep the robot moving straight we are using a combination of a gyroscope and lidar array to measure the exact angle and speed the robot moves at in order to avoid it veering off course or getting stuck. To power it we will use a rechargeable lithium ion battery which will be easy to use for any worker.  All of these features are controlled through the ESP32 microprocessor onboard the robot. Route programming, timing, and results reporting will be handled through a companion app on the operator’s phone or tablet.   

The project functionality will be divided between three teammates. Max will be responsible for the treads, wheel encoder motor, and gyroscope, focusing on the movement, distance measurement, and orientation. Will will be responsible for image recognition, improved camera, and lidar sensor, focusing on collecting visual information and detecting useful information about the condition of the pipe. Kurt will be responsible for the contact switch, rechargeable battery, and power supply, while also working with will on transferring collected results wirelessly to an operator’s phone or computer. The backup string and long, low-profile chassis will be a team effort because these features affect the overall physical design and integration of the robot. 

The robot will not depend on an internet connection while operating underground. Instead , the ESP32 will collect and store the inspection information as the robot travels through the pipe. Afterward, the collected results can be wirelessly transferred to a nearby phone or computer for the operator to review. This allows the robot to work underground without requiring any internet connection. The human-machine interface will primarily be through an app, with collected visual data and time stamps that allows the operator to examine any issues in the pipes. The robot will also have a physical power switch and a button or switch to deploy the robot for inspection. The sensors and actuator system will allow the ESP32 to control the robot’s movement and response to information from the sensors.

The robot will provide several cues to make it easier to use. The app can display information such as battery level, distance traveled, inspection status, and sensor readings. Clearly labeled buttons will help the operator understand what each function does. Warnings can notify the operator when the robot encounters an obstruction or any other problems within the pipe. The app will guide the user through the inspection process and any route programming needed for their pipe system.
